\section{Curved objects and tessellation}
\label{sec:curved}
\here{1}
Curved surfaces are drawn as polygons. The silhouette error of a sphere with $s$ slices is the sagitta of one chord;
the triangle count of \code{createSphere(s, k)} is $2s(k-1)$. Halving the error costs about twice the triangles.
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon=r\Big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{s}\Big)\approx\frac{r\pi^2}{2s^2},\qquad N_\triangle=2s(k-1)
\label{eq:tess}
\end{equation}

Elsewhere: tyres and muzzle rings are tori, helmets and the bunker's berm hemispheres, rotor blur discs flat cylinders,
and the walking and patrol paths are curves too (Catmull--Rom splines, Section~\ref{sec:paths}).

\textbf{Curves.} Paths are curves through points (\code{math/Spline.cpp}, \code{ai/HideController.cpp}):
\begin{gather}
t_{i+1}=t_i+\lVert\vect P_{i+1}-\vect P_i\rVert^{\alpha},\ \alpha=0.5;\qquad
\vect P(u)=h_{00}(u)\vect P_1+h_{10}(u)\vect m_1+h_{01}(u)\vect P_2+h_{11}(u)\vect m_2,\ u\in[0,1]\label{eq:catmull}\\
\vect m_1=(t_2-t_1)\Big(\frac{\vect P_1-\vect P_0}{t_1-t_0}-\frac{\vect P_2-\vect P_0}{t_2-t_0}+\frac{\vect P_2-\vect P_1}{t_2-t_1}\Big)\nonumber\\
h_{00}=2u^3-3u^2+1,\quad h_{10}=u^3-2u^2+u,\quad h_{01}=-2u^3+3u^2,\quad h_{11}=u^3-u^2\nonumber\\
\text{Chaikin: } \vect Q_{2i}=\tfrac34\vect P_i+\tfrac14\vect P_{i+1},\quad \vect Q_{2i+1}=\tfrac14\vect P_i+\tfrac34\vect P_{i+1}\quad(\text{3 passes, end points kept})\nonumber
\end{gather}
\begin{itemize}
\item Centripetal Catmull--Rom ($\alpha=0.5$, soldiers' routes) passes through every waypoint with a continuous tangent and,
unlike the uniform form ($\alpha=0$), never loops or forms a cusp between close points (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}a);
$\vect m_2$ is $\vect m_1$ with every index raised by one.
\item Chaikin's corner cutting (rovers' guide lines) doubles the points per pass and converges to a quadratic B-spline; it
does not pass through the inner points (Figure~\ref{fig:curves}b).
\item Arc length: a table of chords per segment, then binary search, so a soldier walks at constant speed along the curve.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limitation.} The primitives have no level of detail: all \nSphereInst{} sphere parts use \nSphereTri{}
triangles each (about \sphereTriTotal{} million triangles), and a distant sphere costs the same as a close one. Choosing
$s$ from the projected size (for example $s\propto\sqrt{\text{pixels}}$) would cut the sphere triangles by a large
factor in the overview shots.

\section{Curved objects and tessellation}
\label{sec:curved}
\textit{Builds on: primitives (\ref{sec:prims}), shading (\ref{sec:shading}).}

Curved surfaces are drawn as polygons. The silhouette error of a sphere with $s$ slices is the sagitta of one chord;
the triangle count of \code{createSphere(s, k)} is $2s(k-1)$. Halving the error costs about twice the triangles.
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon=r\Big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{s}\Big)\approx\frac{r\pi^2}{2s^2},\qquad N_\triangle=2s(k-1)
\label{eq:tess}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Curved objects in the scene.} Tank road wheels and tyres, barrels, masts, helmets and heads (spheres and
cylinders); the bunker's earth berm (a flattened sphere); rotor blur discs (flat cylinders); the soldiers' walking
paths and the patrol routes are curves too (centripetal Catmull--Rom splines, Section~\ref{sec:paths}). The CC0
trees (v9) carry their own detail levels: three simplified meshes (meshoptimizer) and an octahedral impostor far away.
\where{src/primitives/Sphere.cpp, Cylinder.cpp; LOD chain in src/rendering/Foliage.cpp}
\critique{There is no torus or helix generator, and the primitives have no level of detail: a distant sphere costs
the same 1224 triangles as a close one. Choosing $s$ from projected size (e.g.\ $s\propto\sqrt{\text{pixels}}$) would cut
the sphere triangles by a large factor in the overview shots.}

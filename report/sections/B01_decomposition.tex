\section{Object decomposition catalogue}
\label{sec:decomp}
The scene is built from \nDesigns{} designs. A design is what one builder function (or one prefab) produces; the
scene places them \nDesignInstances{} times, and the instances together hold all \sceneParts{} parts.
Figure~\ref{fig:atlas} shows every design and Table~\ref{tab:objs} counts its parts by primitive. The rest of the
section gives one card per design, family by family.

Every card is read the same way. The left panel is the full model; the right panel draws the same parts flat in the
colour of their primitive and pulls them apart: sub-assemblies first, then the parts inside them. Pale parts belong to a
nested design that has its own card (a camouflage net built over the bunker ramp, the rounds on the UAV's rails).
A dagger ($^\dagger$) marks a part that is hidden at the start, such as a muzzle flash or a missile still in its tube;
the right panel draws it at its rest scale. The legend under the panels counts the design's own parts by primitive and
matches the parts table and the design's row in Table~\ref{tab:objs}.

The parts table lists every part kind. Repeated parts share one row with $\times n$ and a pattern for their position:
$\pm a$ is a mirrored pair, $a{+}di$ counts copies $i=0,1,\dots$ spaced by $d$, $\{a,b\}$ lists distinct values and
$a..b$ gives a range. Positions are in the design's own frame in centimetres; rotations are the part's own $R$ relative
to its parent, in degrees. Where a builder places its parts in a loop, the rule is stated as a formula above the table.

The gait writes only the joint angles of Figure~\ref{fig:soldier-graph}. The phase advances by distance over stride, so
a stance foot stands still on the ground at any speed. During the stance the ankle moves back relative to the hip;
during the swing it follows a cycloid forward with a sine lift. Two-link inverse kinematics then gives the hip and knee
angles that put the ankle there (thigh $L_1=\gait{thigh}$\,m, shin $L_2=\gait{shin}$\,m):
\begin{gather}
x=x_b+(x_f-x_b)\Big(s-\frac{\sin2\pi s}{2\pi}\Big),\quad y=y_0+h\sin\pi s+r(1-s)^2\qquad\text{(swing, }s\in[0,1],\ r=\text{heel rise})\nonumber\\
\kappa=\pi-\arccos\frac{L_1^2+L_2^2-d^2}{2L_1L_2},\quad
\eta=\operatorname{atan2}(x,-y)+\arccos\frac{L_1^2+d^2-L_2^2}{2L_1d},\quad d=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}
\label{eq:gait}
\end{gather}
with $\kappa$ the knee bend and $\eta$ the hip angle (applied as $R_x(-\eta)$ and $R_x(\kappa)$). Walking uses
\gait{speed}\,m/s, stride \gait{stride}\,m, stance \gait{duty} of the cycle, lift $h=\gait{lift}$\,m and a pelvis bob of
\gait{bob}\,m (\gait{cadence_steps_per_min} steps per minute).

\needspace{15cm}\subsection{Vegetation}
Five tree and shrub designs are built from primitives. At ULTRA and EPIC the renderer swaps four of them, and the
boulders, for imported CC0 meshes from Poly Haven; each card names the mesh and its triangle count. Those meshes are
not primitive-built and appear in no table of this section. The cut branches are never swapped.

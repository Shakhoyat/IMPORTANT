\section{Hierarchical modelling and the scene graph}
\label{sec:hier}
\here{2}
Articulated objects are trees. Moving a node moves everything below it, because each world matrix is the
parent's world matrix times the local one. Animation then only changes a few local angles.
\begin{equation}
W_{\text{node}} = W_{\text{parent}}\,M_{\text{node}} = M_{\text{root}}\,M_1\,M_2\cdots M_{\text{node}}
\label{eq:world}
\end{equation}

The camera and the searchlight hang under two gimbal groups. The visual-servo controller writes only their two
angles, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:world} carries the lamp, its light direction and the camera frame along. Section~\ref{sec:decomp}
pulls every design apart along this tree: in Figure~\ref{fig:d-autonomous_uav} the sub-assemblies (motor mount with
rotor, gimbal with camera) move out as blocks before their parts separate.

\textbf{Limitation.} The world matrices of all \nLeaves{} parts are recomputed on the CPU every tick, static
buildings included. That is cheap here (the simulation step takes a few milliseconds); a larger scene would cache static
subtrees.

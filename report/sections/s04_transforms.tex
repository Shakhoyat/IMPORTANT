\section{Transformations and Hierarchies}\label{sec:transform}
The model matrix of a node and its world matrix are
\begin{equation}
  M = T(\vect t)\,R_y(\psi)\,R_x(\theta)\,R_z(\varphi)\,S(\vect s),\qquad
  W_\text{node} = W_\text{parent}\,M ,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  T=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&t_x\\0&1&0&t_y\\0&0&1&t_z\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\;
  S=\begin{bmatrix}s_x&0&0&0\\0&s_y&0&0\\0&0&s_z&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\;
  R_y(\psi)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\psi&0&\sin\psi&0\\0&1&0&0\\-\sin\psi&0&\cos\psi&0\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The primitive is scaled first, then rotated about its own centre, then placed; the other order would scale the
translation too (test T14: $(1,0,0)\xrightarrow{S(2)}(2,0,0)\xrightarrow{R_z(90^\circ)}(0,2,0)\xrightarrow{T(1,2,3)}(1,4,3)$).
Normals use $(M^{-1})^T$ (T15, T16). The same rule is applied at every level: a leaf inherits the product of all its
ancestors' $M$. The animation code of every v4 object only writes the \emph{local} transform of a few group nodes
(for instance a track link's translation, a blade's flap angle, a door's $x$ offset); \code{Scene::updateWorld()}
then recomputes all world matrices top-down in one pass. The pipeline of one vertex is
\begin{equation}
  \vect p_\text{clip}=P\,V\,W_\text{parent}\,T R S\,\vect p_\text{local}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Track link L17 (on the front arc).} With $R_x(\varphi)$ turning the link's local $+y$ onto the outward
normal of the track path ($\varphi=56.74^\circ$, $\cos\varphi=0.548$, $\sin\varphi=0.836$):
\begin{equation}
M=\begin{bmatrix}0.55&0&0&0\\0&0.08\cos\varphi&-0.33\sin\varphi&0.8242\\0&0.08\sin\varphi&0.33\cos\varphi&3.2181\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}0.55&0&0&0\\0&0.0439&-0.2760&0.8242\\0&0.0669&0.1810&3.2181\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
which equals the exported \code{local\_matrix} to the printed digits.

\paragraph{Radar array tile c2 r2.} Column $k$ of the 7 columns sits on an arc of radius $R_a=5$\,m at angle
$\theta_k=(k-4)\cdot7.5^\circ$: $T_x=R_a\sin\theta_k$, $T_z=R_a(1-\cos\theta_k)$ and $R_y=-\theta_k$ (for $k=2$,
$\theta=-15^\circ$, giving $T=(-1.294,0,0.17)$ and $R_y=15^\circ$):
\begin{equation}
M=T R_y(15^\circ)S=\begin{bmatrix}0.64\cos15^\circ&0&0.05\sin15^\circ&-1.294\\0&0.78&0&0\\-0.64\sin15^\circ&0&0.05\cos15^\circ&0.17\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}0.618&0&0.0129&-1.294\\0&0.78&0&0\\-0.166&0&0.0483&0.17\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Rover wheel and why $R_z$ is the spin.} Because $M=T R_y R_x R_z S$, a cylinder (axis $z$) is scaled
first, then spun about its own axis by $R_z$, \emph{then} turned onto the $x$ axis by $R_y(90^\circ)$:
\begin{equation}
M_\text{wheel}=T\,R_y(90^\circ)\,R_z(\omega t)\,S=
\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0.07&0.25\\0&0.16&0&0.08\\-0.16&0&0&-0.22\\0&0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\quad(\omega t=0).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A deep chain: the helicopter blade.}
\begin{equation}
W_\text{blade}=M_\text{root}M_\text{body}M_\text{head}M_\text{tpp}(\tau_f,\tau_l)\,R_y(\Psi)\,M_\text{pivot}(\psi_i)\,
M_\text{flap}(-\beta,\vartheta)\,M_\text{blade},
\end{equation}
with the root carrying world position and yaw, the body the damped-spring pitch and roll, the tip-path-plane node
the cyclic tilts $(\tau_f,\tau_l)$, the spin node the rotor azimuth $\Psi$, the pivot the blade's $72^\circ$ offset,
and the flap node the coning/cyclic flap $\beta$ and the collective feather $\vartheta$ (Section~\ref{sec:control}).

\subsection{The explode view}
Key \texttt{E} pushes the parts of the \emph{bunker, UAV and rover} (the three v1 composite objects) apart:
\code{updateNode()} multiplies the translation of every explodable node by $1+0.7\,e$ with $e\in[0,1]$ eased in by the
application, so at $e=1$ each translation is 1.7 times longer. Only the local translation is scaled, so the children
follow their parent's scaled position and a rigid sub-assembly (a wheel with its hub) opens up as a hierarchy rather than
a cloud. The v4 machines (tank, helicopter, AA gun, radar, base) are not exploded: their transforms are written by
their own animation code every frame. The exploded views for the UAV, rover and bunker are Fig.~\ref{fig:explode}.

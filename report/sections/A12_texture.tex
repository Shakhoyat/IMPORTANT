\section{Texture mapping}
\label{sec:tex}
\here{9}
\concept{A texture adds detail without geometry. A surface point gets coordinates $(u,v)$ (stored per vertex, or
computed from position), the texture is sampled there with filtering and mip-maps, and the result modulates a
material property. A procedural texture computes the value with a function instead of an image.}
\begin{equation}
\text{bump: }\ \vect N'=\mathrm{normalize}\big(\vect N-\nabla h\big),\qquad
\text{mip level: }\ \lambda=\log_2\max\Big(\Big\lVert\tfrac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial x}\Big\rVert,\Big\lVert\tfrac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial y}\Big\rVert\Big)
\label{eq:tex}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Where coordinates come from.} Primitives carry no UV set. Their surfaces (concrete, camouflage, sandbags,
wood, metal, terrain soil and grass) are procedural: noise and cellular patterns evaluated from the world position
in the fragment shader (\code{surfaceAlbedo}, \code{surfaceBump}, \code{terrainShade}). The v9 vegetation assets
bring real UV-mapped image textures (glTF, \code{stb\_image}).
\where{surfaceAlbedo / surfaceBump / terrainShade in src/rendering/ShaderSources.cpp; image textures in src/rendering/Foliage.cpp, Grass.cpp}
\critique{Procedural world-space patterns need no UV unwrapping and never stretch, but they slide over a moving
part unless evaluated in object space, and they cannot be mip-mapped; aliasing of fine noise is left to MSAA and TAA.}

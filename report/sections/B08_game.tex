\section{Game mode}
\label{sec:game}
\textit{Builds on: ray casting (\ref{sec:rt}), the thermal pipeline (\ref{sec:thermal}), paths (\ref{sec:paths}).}

At night intruder rovers attack from the perimeter in waves; two UAVs patrol, detect them in their thermal images,
hold a lock (lead \gv{kSquadLeadLock}\,s, wing \gv{kSquadWingLock}\,s) and launch guided rounds. The player can join in by taking control of a soldier, a tank, the AA
gun or a launcher. Hits are ray tests: each tick a projectile's path segment (previous to current position) is
intersected with every target's bounding sphere and with the static solids' boxes, so a fast round cannot tunnel
through a target between two ticks; an explosion then damages targets inside its blast radius.
The squad never reads a rover's position from the scene: everything it knows comes out of its own two cameras,
through the chain of Figure~\ref{fig:cvchain}, so the game is a working computer-vision system on top of the renderer.

\subsection{How the two UAVs work together}
The two UAVs do not send each other messages; they share one \emph{track picture}. Every frame, both UAVs' ground
measurements go into one multi-target tracker; the squad logic then reads that picture and gives each UAV a role.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Predict} each track with a constant-velocity Kalman filter, state $(x,z,v_x,v_z)$, process noise \gv{accelNoise}\,m/s$^2$.
\item \textbf{Gate}: a measurement may belong to a track only if its squared Mahalanobis distance
$d^2=\boldsymbol\nu^\top S^{-1}\boldsymbol\nu<\gv{gateChi2}$ ($\chi^2$, 2 degrees of freedom, 99\,\%).
\item \textbf{Assign} measurements to tracks with the Hungarian algorithm (minimum total $d^2$), one camera at a time.
\item \textbf{Triangulate}: a rover seen by both UAVs in the same frame gives two skew pixel rays; the fused point is the
midpoint of their shortest connecting segment, far more accurate than one ray hitting the ground.
\item \textbf{Life cycle}: tentative $\to$ confirmed after \gv{confirmHits} hits (event ``enemy spotted'') $\to$ coasting
after \gv{coastAfter}\,s unseen $\to$ deleted after \gv{deleteAfter}\,s.
\end{itemize}

With a confirmed target (the track most threatening to a camp asset), the UAV with the longer lock becomes
\textbf{LEAD} and keeps its bearing; the other becomes \textbf{WING} and flies about $90^\circ$ around the target, at
\gv{kSquadStandOff}\,m stand-off and \gv{kSquadEngageAlt}\,m height: two viewing directions at right angles give the best
triangulation and a second attack axis. When the lead has held the target \gv{kSquadLeadLock}\,s and the wing
\gv{kSquadWingLock}\,s, they make a \textbf{combined strike} timed so both rounds arrive together (time on target): each
round's flight time $T_k$ is predicted from the motor model (\gv{flight_launch_speed}\,m/s at launch, \gv{flight_cruise_speed}\,m/s after
\gv{flight_ramp_time}\,s), the farther UAV launches first and the nearer one waits $T_{\mathrm{far}}-T_k$; each round homes on the target's
predicted position at its own arrival. If the wing cannot lock within \gv{kSquadWingWait}\,s, the lead strikes alone
after \gv{kSquadSoloLock}\,s. A target lost behind cover is searched for \gv{kSquadReacquireTime}\,s: both UAVs circle its last
known position on opposite sides and different radii to look around the occluder.
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{launch},k}=t_0+\big(\max_j T_j-T_k\big),\qquad
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{track}}+\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{track}}\max_j T_j
\label{eq:tot}
\end{equation}

The rounds themselves steer by proportional navigation: the turn rate is
$\boldsymbol\omega=4\,|\mathbf{v}|\,(\hat{\mathbf{v}}\times\mathbf{r})/|\mathbf{r}|^2+2\,\hat{\mathbf{v}}\times\hat{\mathbf{r}}$,
clamped to \gv{kMissileTurnRate}\,rad/s, and the velocity is turned each tick by Rodrigues' rotation about
$\hat{\boldsymbol\omega}$ --- the same axis--angle rotation as in Section~\ref{sec:trs}.

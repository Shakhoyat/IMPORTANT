\section{Game mode}
\label{sec:game}
\here{10}
At night intruder rovers attack in waves; two \ab{583}{UAVs} patrol, find them in their thermal images, lock on and launch guided
rounds, and the player can take over a soldier, a tank, the \ab{584}{AA} gun or a launcher. The squad never reads a rover's
position from the scene: everything it knows comes from its own two cameras (Figure~\ref{fig:cvchain}). Hits are ray
tests of each projectile's path segment per tick against target spheres and solid boxes, so a fast round cannot tunnel.

\subsection{How the two UAVs (uncrewed aerial vehicles) work together}
The two \ab{585}{UAVs} do not send each other messages; they share one \emph{track picture}. Every frame, both \ab{586}{UAVs}' ground
measurements go into one multi-target tracker; the squad logic then reads that picture and gives each \ab{587}{UAV} a role.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Predict} each track with a constant-velocity Kalman filter, state $(x,z,v_x,v_z)$, process noise \gv{accelNoise}\,m/s$^2$.
\item \textbf{Gate}: a measurement may belong to a track only if its squared Mahalanobis distance
$d^2=\boldsymbol\nu^\top S^{-1}\boldsymbol\nu<\gv{gateChi2}$ ($\chi^2$, 2 degrees of freedom, 99\,\%).
\item \textbf{Assign} measurements to tracks with the Hungarian algorithm (minimum total $d^2$), one camera at a time.
\item \textbf{Triangulate}: a rover seen by both \ab{588}{UAVs} in the same frame gives two skew pixel rays; the fused point is the
midpoint of their shortest connecting segment, far more accurate than one ray hitting the ground.
\item \textbf{Life cycle}: tentative $\to$ confirmed after \gv{confirmHits} hits (event ``enemy spotted'') $\to$ coasting
after \gv{coastAfter}\,s unseen $\to$ deleted after \gv{deleteAfter}\,s.
\end{itemize}

On a confirmed target the \ab{589}{UAV} with the longer lock becomes \textbf{LEAD}; the other, \textbf{WING}, flies about
$90^\circ$ around the target at \gv{kSquadStandOff}\,m stand-off for the best triangulation. After \gv{kSquadLeadLock}\,s
(lead) and \gv{kSquadWingLock}\,s (wing) of lock they make a \textbf{combined strike}: each round's flight time $T_k$ is
predicted, the farther \ab{590}{UAV} launches first and both rounds arrive together (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tot}). A target lost behind
cover is searched for \gv{kSquadReacquireTime}\,s by circling its last known position from both sides.
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{launch},k}=t_0+\big(\max_j T_j-T_k\big),\qquad
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{track}}+\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{track}}\max_j T_j
\label{eq:tot}
\end{equation}

The rounds themselves steer by proportional navigation: the turn rate is
$\boldsymbol\omega=4\,|\mathbf{v}|\,(\hat{\mathbf{v}}\times\mathbf{r})/|\mathbf{r}|^2+2\,\hat{\mathbf{v}}\times\hat{\mathbf{r}}$,
clamped to \gv{kMissileTurnRate}\,rad/s, and the velocity is turned each tick by Rodrigues' rotation about
$\hat{\boldsymbol\omega}$ --- the same axis--angle rotation as in Section~\ref{sec:trs}.

\subsection{Single strike, combined strike and the guards}
\label{sec:coop}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Combined strike.} One intruder is engaged by both \ab{591}{UAVs}: the lead waits up to 8\,s for the wing's lock,
then both rounds are timed to arrive together.
\item \textbf{Single strike.} A second intruder more than 14\,m from the first and within 10\,m of a protected point
cannot wait. The \ab{592}{UAV} nearer to it leaves the pair and strikes it with one round; the other \ab{593}{UAV} strikes the first target
alone. When the second intruder is destroyed or lost the pair forms again.
\item \textbf{Guards.} A soldier standing at his post with a tracked intruder closer than 26\,m and a clear line turns to
it and fires bursts of 0.6\,s every 2.4\,s. The same segment test as the \ab{594}{UAVs}' rounds decides the clear line.
\item \textbf{Weapons on alert.} With nobody at the controls the launchers keep their canisters and pod raised through
the apertures of their nets and sweep their sector, the helicopter's rotor turns at flight idle, and the tank and the
anti-aircraft gun lay on the tracked intruder (\code{WorldContext::alert}, \code{threatPos}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Getting into the vehicles}
\label{sec:board}
A soldier under the player's control walks up to the tank, the anti-aircraft gun, a launcher or the helicopter and
presses \key{E}. He is no longer drawn (\code{Soldier::setAboard}), the vehicle takes the player's input, and \key{C}
switches between the chase camera and the gunner's sight. \key{E} or \key{Esc} puts him back on his feet at the first
free spot beside the vehicle: eight directions on up to three rings are tested against the static boxes and the
vehicle's own footprint (\code{App::boardVehicle}, \code{App::exitVehicle}). The vehicle then returns to its alert
behaviour.

\textbf{Limitation.} The guards fire only from their posts, not while walking a route. The garrison works two shifts
(day 06:24--17:36 and night) with staggered changes.

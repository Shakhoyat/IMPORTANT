\section{Rasterisation and anti-aliasing}
\label{sec:raster}
\here{7}
\concept{Rasterisation finds the pixels whose centres a triangle covers (edge functions) and interpolates the
vertex attributes with barycentric weights. Because the triangle was projected, attributes must be interpolated
perspective-correctly: interpolate $a/w$ and $1/w$, then divide.}
\begin{equation}
E_{ij}(\vect x)=(\vect x-\vect p_i)\times(\vect p_j-\vect p_i),\quad
\lambda_k=\frac{E_k}{E_0+E_1+E_2},\qquad
a=\frac{\sum_k\lambda_k a_k/w_k}{\sum_k\lambda_k/w_k}
\label{eq:raster}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Anti-aliasing.} Edges sampled once per pixel form stairs. The program uses MSAA (several coverage samples, one
shading per pixel): 2$\times$ at LOW, 4$\times$ at HIGH, 8$\times$ at ULTRA, and 4$\times$ plus TAA at EPIC. TAA
jitters the projection by a Halton(2,3) sub-pixel offset every frame and blends the history, clipped to the
variance box of the current $3\times3$ neighbourhood in YCoCg (\code{ShadersPostFx.cpp kTaaFrag}).

\critique{TAA trades aliasing for ghosting: a fast UAV leaves a faint trail for a few frames. The v9 vegetation
work measured that, with a static camera, pixels without vegetation still change by about 2.4/255 per frame from the
jitter alone, and vegetation pixels by 2.6--6.6/255 (target 1.5; not met, \code{docs/v9\_notes/v9-notes.md}).}

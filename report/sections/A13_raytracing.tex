\section{Ray tracing concepts in the project}
\label{sec:rt}
\here{10}
A ray $\vect r(t)=\vect o+t\vect d$ is intersected with the scene. Rays answer three questions here: what is
under the cursor (picking), is a point lit (shadow rays), and what does a mirror or glass show (reflection and
refraction rays, Whitted \cite{whitted1980}). A \ab{556}{CPU}/GPU ray tracer with a \ab{557}{BVH} runs over the same scene.
\begin{equation}
\text{sphere: } t^2(\vect d\cdot\vect d)+2t\,\vect d\cdot(\vect o-\vect c)+\lVert\vect o-\vect c\rVert^2-r^2=0;\quad
\text{plane: } t=\frac{(\vect p_0-\vect o)\cdot\vect n}{\vect d\cdot\vect n}
\label{eq:rays}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\vect r=\vect d-2(\vect d\cdot\vect n)\vect n,\qquad
\vect t=\eta\,\vect d+\big(\eta\cos\theta_i-\sqrt{1-\eta^2(1-\cos^2\theta_i)}\big)\vect n,\ \eta=\tfrac{n_1}{n_2}
\label{eq:reflref}
\end{equation}
\textbf{The intersection routines} (\code{raytrace/Intersect.cpp}, \code{Heightfield.cpp}, \code{RtScene.cpp}). Every part is
a unit shape under its world matrix $W$, so the ray is moved into the part's own frame instead of moving the shape:
\begin{gather}
\vect o'=W^{-1}\vect o,\quad \vect d'=W^{-1}\vect d\ \ (w=0),\qquad \vect n_{\text{world}}=\operatorname{normalize}\big((W^{-1})^\top\vect n'\big)\label{eq:rtobj}\\
\text{box (slabs): } t_{\text{near}}=\max_a\min\Big(\frac{-\frac12-o_a}{d_a},\frac{\frac12-o_a}{d_a}\Big),\quad
t_{\text{far}}=\min_a\max(\cdot),\quad \text{hit iff } t_{\text{near}}\le t_{\text{far}}\nonumber\\
\text{cylinder side: } (o_x+td_x)^2+(o_y+td_y)^2=\tfrac14,\ |o_z+td_z|\le\tfrac12;\qquad
\text{caps: } t=\frac{\pm\frac12-o_z}{d_z},\ x^2+y^2\le\tfrac14\nonumber\\
\text{triangle (M\"oller--Trumbore): } \vect e_1=\vect b-\vect a,\quad \vect e_2=\vect c-\vect a,\quad \vect P=\vect d\times\vect e_2,\quad \vect Q=(\vect o-\vect a)\times\vect e_1\nonumber\\
u=\frac{(\vect o-\vect a)\cdot\vect P}{\vect e_1\cdot\vect P},\quad v=\frac{\vect d\cdot\vect Q}{\vect e_1\cdot\vect P},\quad
t=\frac{\vect e_2\cdot\vect Q}{\vect e_1\cdot\vect P},\qquad\text{hit iff } u,v\ge0,\ u+v\le1\nonumber
\end{gather}
\begin{gather}
F_{\text{exact}}=\tfrac12\big(r_s^2+r_p^2\big),\quad r_s=\frac{\eta\cos\theta_i-\cos\theta_t}{\eta\cos\theta_i+\cos\theta_t},\quad
r_p=\frac{\cos\theta_i-\eta\cos\theta_t}{\cos\theta_i+\eta\cos\theta_t}\label{eq:fresnelexact}\\
F_{\text{Schlick}}=F_0+(1-F_0)(1-\cos\theta)^5,\qquad \text{total internal reflection iff } \eta^2(1-\cos^2\theta_i)>1\nonumber\\
\text{surface area heuristic (SAH): } C=C_t+C_i\,\frac{A_L\,n_L+A_R\,n_R}{A_P},\qquad C_t=1,\ C_i=1.5,\ 16 \text{ bins per axis, leaves of }\le4\label{eq:sah}
\end{gather}
\begin{itemize}
\item Quadratics are solved in the stable form $q=-(b+\operatorname{sign}(b)\sqrt{b^2-ac})$, roots $q/a$ and $c/q$, from the point of
closest approach, so a 1\,cm wire 60\,m away keeps its precision.
\item The terrain is a height field of triangles walked cell by cell with the 3D \ab{558}{DDA} of Section~\ref{sec:raster}; glass uses
the exact Fresnel term with $\eta=1/1.5$ entering and $1.5$ leaving; smooth metal and wet surfaces use Schlick with
$F_0=\operatorname{mix}(0.04,\boldsymbol\rho,m)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:fresnel}).
\item The \ab{559}{BVH} split minimises $C$ over the 15 bin boundaries of each axis: $A$ are box surface areas, $n$ primitive counts.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Uses in the game.} Mouse picking casts a ray through the clicked pixel (middle click jumps into that object);
the interaction prompt tests a segment against wall boxes for line of sight; the \ab{560}{UAV} searchlight's beam length is a
ray marched to the terrain and refined by bisection; a third-person camera arm is a sphere cast against the walls.

\textbf{Limitation.} The ray-traced modes are teaching modes: at 1080p the path tracer runs at reduced internal resolution
(0.5) and accumulates over frames, so it is not used in play. The raster renderer approximates reflections with
screen-space reflections (Section~\ref{sec:rtadv}).

\section{Colour interpolation, with critique}
\label{sec:color}
\textit{Builds on: rasterisation (\ref{sec:raster}), shading (\ref{sec:shading}), illumination (\ref{sec:illum}).}

Colour is interpolated in four places: across a triangle (barycentric), between shading models (Gouraud vs Phong),
between colour spaces (sRGB vs linear), and over time (sky and fog presets over the day). Each has a naive version
that fails visibly.
\begin{equation}
c_{\text{lin}}=\begin{cases}c_s/12.92 & c_s\le0.04045\\ \big(\frac{c_s+0.055}{1.055}\big)^{2.4} & \text{else}\end{cases},\qquad
\text{mixLog}(a,b,t)=\exp\big((1-t)\ln a+t\ln b\big)
\label{eq:srgb}
\end{equation}

\where{toLinear() and the output encoding in src/rendering/ShaderSources.cpp; mixLog() in src/scene/DayCycle.cpp; Frame Explorer interpolation stage}
\critique{Log-space blending (\code{mixLog}, base 2 in the code) is undefined for a zero channel: every preset
must stay strictly positive, and a preset with a near-zero channel makes the blend linger near black for most of the
transition. Linear-space blending is right for light, but the result is still stored in 8 bits per channel; the only
defence against banding in dark gradients is the film grain added in the final pass (\code{ShadersPostFx.cpp}).}

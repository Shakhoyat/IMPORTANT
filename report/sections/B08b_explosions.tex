\section{Explosions: particles, physics and colour}
\label{sec:explode}
\here{7}
An explosion is a particle system: one call spawns a few hundred short-lived particles of seven kinds. Each tick moves
every particle by its own law of motion (Table~\ref{tab:explode}); each frame draws it as one instanced quad, facing the
camera, stretched along its velocity or laid on the ground. Its colour is computed in the shader from a temperature $T$.

\paragraph{Physics.} Drag is an exact decay, $\mathbf{v}\leftarrow\mathbf{v}\,e^{-k\Delta t}$ (frame-rate independent);
fire and smoke rise, sparks fall and bounce, and the rover's own primitives become debris.

\paragraph{Colour from temperature.} Scrolling fractal noise gives a temperature field
$T=\mathrm{clamp}(1-d)\,(1-t)^{\gv{fireLife}}$, mapped by a blackbody-like ramp in which red rises first, then green, then blue:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{bb}(T)=\big(T^{\gv{bbR}},\ T^{\gv{bbG}},\ \gv{bbBk}\,T^{\gv{bbB}}\big),\qquad
\mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{fire}}=\mathrm{bb}(T)\,(\gv{fireEmitA}+\gv{fireEmitB}\,T^2)
\label{eq:bb}
\end{equation}
A cooling point slides from white-yellow through orange and deep red to black: interpolation along a curve through RGB
space. The emission is high dynamic range (HDR), so bloom makes it glow. Blending is premultiplied (emission adds light, soot hides what is
behind), with the smoke group and the glowing group each sorted back to front.

\textbf{Limitation.} Sorting is per group, not across the two groups: a glowing particle behind a smoke puff is drawn after
the puff and so shows through it (bright additive light makes this hard to notice). Sparks and embers bounce on
the terrain only, not on buildings.
